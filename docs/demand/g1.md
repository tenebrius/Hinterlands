# Growth rule (C), D182: the sources, S0 and S1 (explorations)

From the implementer, 24–25 October 2026. Explorations only: 15 runs for S0 and 9 a variant for S1 (D146). Nothing here is a gate result.

## 1. The sources, stated before S1
**Domesday's share:**
- **1290: 4.75 million** (Broadberry, Campbell, Klein, Overton & van Leeuwen 2010, *British Economic Growth 1270–1870*, CAGE working paper, Table 18; read). The same table gives 4.81 million in 1348 and 4.69 in 1315.
- **1086: 1.71 million**, the 2015 book's estimate. It is reported as such in reviews; the table itself was not read here.
- **`settled_phi` = 1.71 / 4.75 = 0.360.**

**Everitt's market density:**
- **The count:** "Alan Everitt's enumeration of 760 English market towns" (British Academy, *British Town Maps*, citing Everitt 1967, "The marketing of agricultural produce", *Agrarian History of England and Wales* IV, pp. 466–592, list pp. 468–75).
  - A secondary use (*Urban History*, "Moral marketplaces", citing Everitt p. 467) gives "the 800 or so small market towns" across England and Wales.
  - The original was not read.
- **England's area:** about 130,300 km² (standard figure; *to check* to the km²).
- **The spacing:**
  - 130,300 / 760 = 171 km² per market;
  - hexagonal spacing d = √(2a/√3) = 14.1 km;
  - England and Wales, 151,000 / 800, gives 14.8 km.
- **The band:** 14.1 ± 25% = 10.5–17.6 km. Bracton's 6⅔ miles (10.7 km) is above the lower edge, so it is the floor. **Gated band: 10.7–17.6 km** among market places in year 500 (validate.rs, D182).
- **Roads** are reported (persistence on routes ±2 cells, the count at ≥ 0.90, net length, abandonment). `PAIRED` is empty.

## 2. S0: the default, measured (`hinterlands growthtrace`; seeds 1–5 × maps 1–3; `target/d2/s0`)
**Only births add people.**
- Founding and failure move people, and their decade sums are 0 in every run.
- The events column is the plague calendar, endemic mortality and the planted towns.

**The fill** (map 1 seed 1):

| Decade from | 1150 | 1200 | 1250 | 1290 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rate a year | 2.5% | 2.4% | 2.4% | 1.4% |

Births per decade peak at about 7,500 in the 1280s, and shedding rises from 1280.

**Land in use is 6% of farmable land at the 1290s** (fields over cells with yield at least `min_field_yield`), 10% by 1600.
- **The region is far from full.** The slowdown before 1348 is local: places fill their own reaches faster than founding (at most 6 × 30 people every five years) opens new ones.
- **The 1340 peak is not a Malthusian ceiling.** The peak year is 1348 in all 15 runs, with the region still growing (1290 → 1348 about 0.7% a year).

**The trigger:**
- the region first reaches 0.36 × its own 1290 level at year 142–145 in all 15 runs;
- its 1290 level is 42.0–45.1k on 11,541–12,384 km² of farmable land, a density of 3.43–3.89 people a km² (median 3.58);
- **`settled_ref_density` = 3.58** (named calibration, D182).

## 3. S1: (C) built, off by default (`settled_start`)
**The build:**
- `Sim::new` runs the spin-up with today's rule and `events_mode` 0, until the population reaches `settled_phi` × `settled_ref_density` × farmable km² (guard `spinup_max_years` 400).
- `State::renumber` then moves every stored year back by the spin-up's length:
  - founded, last fed, grass logged, reach refreshed, famine;
  - the weather already used is dropped;
  - the founding cache is cleared;
  - year 0 is 1100.
- Every caller (CLI, validate, viewer) works unchanged.
- After the spin-up, births follow the place's own food ratio at most `r_grow_pc` (1%).
- With the switch off the golden is unchanged (`6db34602d4431473`).

## 4. The three causes (decade decomposition, v1 and v5, map 1 seed 1)
**(a) The peak comes at the famine.**
- The famine sheds 19% of the people in 1315–17 (9,877 of 50.9k).
- At settled rates (about 0.6–0.7% a year realised) the region regains only about 14% by 1340.
- The record was nearly flat: 4.69 million in 1315, 4.81 in 1348 (Broadberry Table 18), and the famine's loss is usually put at 10–15% (Jordan 1996; *to check*).
- Under the frontier rate the default regained it in 15 years, which hid the depth.

**(b) The sourced calendar and endemic 0.007 count the same deaths twice.**
- **v1, 1350–1450:** births run at 0.9–1.0% a year, close to the cap, while deaths run at about 1.7% a year (endemic 0.7% and the sourced outbreaks about 1.0%). The region falls about 1.0% a year, against the record's 0.3% (2.60 million in 1351, 1.90 in 1450).
- **With endemic 0 (v5)** Hatcher is 0.50–0.54, inside the band.
- **Reading:** Hatcher's fifteenth-century excess mortality, the "sourced part" of endemic, comes from monastic series (Christ Church Canterbury and others; Hatcher 1986, *to check*) that include the plague years. The sourced calendar now applies those outbreaks year by year. So endemic's sourced part and the calendar overlap.

**(c) Births cannot answer the land.**
- Realised births are only a fraction of the cap:
  - 0.3–0.6% a year in 1200–1290;
  - 0.3–0.5% in 1510–1590 (v5).
- This holds while 2–6% of farmable land is in use.
- **The cause is the cultivation rule** (D162). Each place claims land to its need × 1.2, so its food ratio sits near 1.05–1.1 whatever land is free. Births ramp from 0 at a ratio of 1 to the cap at 1.2, so they run at about a third of the cap.
- **Under the default** the same ramp at 2.7% gives about 0.65% realised, which is why the default's recovery looked right.
- **Under (C)** the 1% cap gives about 0.3%, and the recovery misses (v5, v6).

## 5. Readings, for the designer (no change made)
1. **(c) is the deepest of the three.** The settled rate cannot be "at most 1% on the food ratio" while the food ratio is held near 1 by need-led claims. Two ways out:
   - **(c1) Births at a sourced settled rate when fed:** `r_settled` whenever the food ratio is at least 1, scaled down only below it (a gate, not a ramp).
     - The rate is sourced: Broadberry 0.50% for 1086–1290, and 0.55–0.60% for 1490–1600 (Table 18).
     - The Malthusian check then works through famine shedding and the hunger tolerance, as now.
     - **Prediction:** 1100–1290 at about 0.5% (1340/1100 ≈ 2.7–3); the famine regained by about 1350; recovery about 0.55% less deaths.
   - **(c2) Births on land per head,** the labour-led land rule (D162/D163), which found land per head already high in 1300. It is a larger change, and I would not take it first.
2. **(b): with (C), endemic's sourced part should be 0 when the sourced calendar is on.** Every death is then counted once, by outbreak. Endemic 0.007 would stay only for the default calendar, whose later outbreaks are not sourced.
3. **(a): the famine's depth (19% shed against the record's 10–15%)** is a separate miss. It was hidden by fast regrowth. I'd report it, not change it, unless (c1) leaves the peak at 1315.
4. **What I'd propose:** pre-register (C) + (c1) + endemic 0 + the sourced calendar, with the same five bands, and run it as S1b, 9 runs. If it meets them, S2 follows.
