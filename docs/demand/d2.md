# D1b + D2: port reach and cloth, gated together (D126). Gate failed; stopped

## Built (both switches off; golden unchanged, `4a9737eafc305561`)
- **`port_reach` (D1b, D125):** the nearest port beyond `c_max` for salt and iron (`docs/demand/d1b.md`).
- **`cloth` (D2):**
  - **The fallow flock:** ⅓ of the arable fallow, 1 sheep a fallow acre (calibration, D124). Its wool is the farmers' own.
  - **Wool and export cloth go by port reach,** the nearest port at the full travel cost. Wool's two-hop rule is replaced.
  - **Mills:** settlements with `mill` and W > 0, with capacity `cloth_lb_per_w` (30 lb) × W. Each bids in two tiers on last year's prices, less the making cost (3.2 d a lb, D126):
    - home: last year's home sales (a tenth more when it sold out), never less than its own people's need, at the better of its home price and export;
    - export: the rest of the capacity, at the world cloth price (Munro, 5.7 d a lb) less the custom and carriage.
  - **The sale:** seller–mill pairs are taken in order of surplus, within capacity and purse. Every seller is paid its export netback, so the mill pays from its market purse.
  - **Home cloth** is a sixth good in goods trade, at 1.5 yards × 2 lb a yard for each adult (0.6 of people) and unit elasticity. Only home-tier cloth is offered there. A town no cloth reaches signals its scarcity by a price rising a tenth a year.
  - **Export cloth:** the export tier and unsold home cloth go to the nearest port. The margin over wool counts to the mill's market inflow.
  - **Duties leave the region as coin (`duties_out`):**
    - the ancient custom of 6s 8d a sack from 1275;
    - the subsidy of 40s from 1342;
    - the cloth custom of 14d a cloth from 1347.
  - **Coin is conserved** (`tests/money.rs`, a second test with both switches on). Outside trade counts export cloth as it counts wool.
- **Validate:**
  - outside trade 3–50% (D126);
  - exports a head c. 1300 reported;
  - stores read by the D124 measure.

## The wool share of a broadcloth's value, and the making cost (D126.4)
- **Munro gives wool's share of a broadcloth's cost.** These are Flemish cloths made of English wool bought at the Calais Staple: Munro, *Hanseatic commerce in textiles from the Low Countries and England, 1290–1570*, MPRA 11199, p. 113.
  - **Leuven 1434, a fine black broadcloth:** wool 62.5% of total cost, 76.2% of pre-finishing costs.
  - **Ypres 1500, a black broadcloth of Cotswold wool:** wool 52.0% of total cost, 64.2% of pre-finishing costs. Dyeing and dressing were 18–19% of total cost.
- **No share for an English-made broadcloth was found.**
- **The implied making cost** at Munro's English export price of 5.7 d a lb, (1 − wool share) × 5.7:
  - on total cost: 2.1 d (Leuven) to 2.7 d (Ypres);
  - on pre-finishing cost: 1.4 d to 2.0 d. English exports were mostly undyed whites, which makes these the closer comparison.
- **Against the bracket 2.62–3.77:**
  - every figure is below it except Ypres on total cost (2.7 d);
  - on the closer comparison (pre-finishing) the implied cost is 1.4–2.0 d, well below.
- **Per D126, read without tuning:** cloth for export would have paid before the subsidy at Munro's price and shares, so something the model lacks held English cloth back before 1340.
- **The caveat runs both ways.**
  - The Flemish wool was dearer than English wool at home: the Staple price was more than double the domestic price by the 1490s. That raises its share.
  - Flemish labour was dearer than English, which lowers it.
- **The making cost stays at 3.2 d** (D126: reported, not tuned).
