# D0: demand from stocks, the diagnostic (D118, D120)

A probe with no feedback, `hinterlands demand`. It is CLI only: the model never reads it, every v7 output and the golden are unchanged, and `cargo test` passes. It is aggregated by `tools/demand/d0.py` over 3 seeds on each generated map (medians), by decade.

Units are each good's existing ones: grain-value units at price 1, one person-year of grain (6 bushels at 0.60 s, or 43.2 d).

## Pre-registered (reported only)
- **Iron falls after 1348 with the arable land: met.** Stock-based iron is 2,718 in 1300–1340 and 1,698 in 1400–1440 (−38%). Arable cells fall from 877 to 533 (−39%). Iron per arable cell is 3.17 in both periods, fixed by construction, since plough iron dominates.
- **Growing places take more than their share of W in stone: met** in 352 of 369 decades from 1200 (e.g. 1300: 0.90 of stone against 0.84 of W; 1400: 0.29 against 0.27).

## Findings
1. **Iron on a stock basis is 30–40 times today's demand.**
   - Today's 0.0013 a person was calibrated (D71) so that iron's price sits at 1–3 against coppiced output. Plough irons and shoeing alone, at Langdon's costs, come to about 3 value units an arable cell, about 0.05 a person.
   - At D1, iron's demand level and the mines' capacity (fuel) would have to be reconciled; the designer's S1 calibration is what keeps the price in range.
   - Villages hold most of it (1,454 of 2,486 in 1300), as ploughs do.
2. **Salt on a stock basis is about 5.6 times today's,** but the per-person figure is a calibration with no source. Bridbury (1955) is the source to consult before D1.
3. **Stone follows growth.** The growth term adds 40–80% to repair in decades of town growth (1300: 491 against 273) and nothing in decades of decline (1350, 1440). Most of it goes to the large towns and the ports. After 1348, small towns (W 100–500) collapse in stone demand (92 → 25).
4. **Cloth sizes D2.**
   - At Dyer's three yards an adult, the region's own cloth would need 3–5 times the wool it produces: 317,000 lb against 76,000 lb in 1300; 214,000 against 85,000 in 1450.
   - Dyer's figure is for 1500 and a richer population. The model's wool, about 2 lb a person, covers less than half of it.
   - So cloth made at home from the region's own wool would leave nothing to export unless cloth per head is set far lower, or the wool per cell (L1) higher.
   - D2's values (cloth per head before and after 1348, and wool per cell) need settling before code. The cloth note will take this up.
5. **Works (mines, salterns, quarries) nearly vanish after 1348** in this run set (stone 26 → 0), so tools per working site barely matter.
