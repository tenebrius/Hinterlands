# v7 M2: marketplaces — first build (stopped)

Built as in `docs/market/m1-design.md` and D88, behind `marketplaces` (off). The code is in `crates/hinterlands-core/src/systems/marketplace.rs`, called from trade. New parameters: `market_min` 50, `toll` 0.03. The golden is unchanged (`1faa5c46e3908124`).

## What was built
- **Level 1.** Rent in kind plus the farmers' surplus (farmers eat first up to `food_cap ×` the need of pop − W) is routed by `W^α exp(−β c)`, with transport loss, as v6's gravity loop. Turnover is what arrives.
- **Level 2.** Markets with `W ≥ market_min`, and the ports, clear by the entropy solver with S2's level step and floor. Each small market clears locally and trades its excess or shortfall with its nearest large market. On map 1 this is about 30–60 nodes.
- **Buyers.** Market people buy `W × min(p^(−ε), food_cap)` within a grain budget of rents sold there, the toll and last year's fees. The ports buy up to the world price. Unsold grain is lost.
- **`market_in`** is the grain sold at the market, so W's inflow is turnover sold, not arrived. Relief and village stores are on, as in v6 W2.

## Why
- **v6's market feedback works through gross inflow.** A large market draws more grain by `W^α`, and the whole arrival counts towards W, so W grows faster than linearly in its pull.
- **Under marketplaces, W grows only on grain sold,** and grain at a market is bought almost only by its own market people (up to `food_cap` each) and the ports. So `T_sold` scales with W itself, and the pull `ε(T_sold − K W)` loses its positive feedback.
- **The pull still sends grain to big markets, but it is unsold there,** and unsold grain does not count. M0 measured turnover arrived, not sold. Arrived turnover ranks like `market_in` (M0); sold turnover does not.
- **This is S2's finding again in another form.** The hierarchy rests on grain that reaches a market whether or not anyone there eats it.

## Options for the designer
- **A. W grows on turnover arrived (grain brought to market), not sold.** The unsold part is lost as now, and counts towards W as the business of the market (handling, storage, brokerage) though nobody eats it. It is M0's quantity, so the hierarchy should return. But it is P3's "burned grain grows W" in a new name.
- **B. M3 first.** Market stores take the unsold grain, and W grows on grain sold plus grain stored. That gives the business a real destination, though stores fill and then the same problem returns.
- **C. Stop v7** under the proposal's own stop rule and keep v6 W2.

`marketplaces` stays off. Stopped.

## Second build (D90: W on grain arriving) — FAILED; stopped
- **Built as decided.** `market_in` is the grain arriving at the market (rent plus surplus), so W follows `ε(A − K W)` on W2's ten-year average. Market people still buy and eat within their income. Unsold grain is lost and reported.
- **The new gated check** `unsold at markets` (≤ 10% of arrivals after year 200) is in `validate`.
- **Untuned (K 1.5, `market_share` 0.4866 × 1.5/3.39 = 0.215), map 1 seed 1:** the region collapses. Population at years 100 / 200 / 300 / 400 / 500 is 1,483 / 3,510 / 2,604 / 5,143 / 5,039, and the largest place is at most 478. The pre-registered hierarchy on ≥ 2 maps cannot be met, so the full untuned validation was not run.
- **Tuning round:** K 1.2 / 1.5 / 2.0 × `market_share` 0.1 / 0.215 / 0.3 × toll 0.03 / 0.10, 18 runs on map 1 seed 1. Population at year 400 is 1,285–18,254 (v6 W2: 103,466), and the largest place at most 1,883. Every setting collapses or stays small, so no gate was run.

**Why.**
- **Every place counts its own sales as arrivals at its own market** (`home_weight`). With K between 1.2 and 2.0, W grows towards arrivals / K in every village, not only in towns.
- **Within 20–30 years W equals the whole population** in most places (map 1 seed 1 at year 30: W = pop in all four largest).
- **Then there are no farmers,** so all the harvest is sold, and the place's people are market people who must buy their food. But their budget is only the rent, the toll and fees, and the farmers' sales revenue is nobody's.
- **They go short and are shed,** harvests fall with the people, and the region collapses.
- **In v6, K = 3.39 kept W at a fraction of each village's people.** Re-read as consumption, K is too low to do that.

## Options for the designer
- **A. Market people's budget includes the farmers' sales revenue of their own place.** When W absorbs a village's farmers, their sales stay income. This closes the accounting gap. W would still reach the whole population everywhere, and the villages would become "markets".
- **B. Cap W at the place's non-farming people:** `pop − (people its fields need)`, with `people_per_prime_cell` and the fields' yield. W then cannot swallow the farmers. This is a physical rule: those who work the fields are farmers.
- **C. Count only arrivals from other places towards W,** without the home market. A village's own sales then do not grow its own market, and only places that draw grain from others grow W.

B and C each address the cause directly. My recommendation is C, possibly with B as a guard, since home sales making every village a market is what failed here.

`marketplaces` stays off, and the golden is unchanged. Stopped.
