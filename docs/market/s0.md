# v6 S0: shadow prices (report)

S0 solves a spatial price equilibrium each year beside the existing trade, for grain and the four goods (salt, iron, stone, imports), and records the prices. Nothing feeds back. It is behind `market_shadow`, off by default. With it off, or on, the model's own state is the same; with it off the golden is unchanged (`4ce58fae34550933`). The method is in `crates/hinterlands-core/src/systems/market.rs` and D69.

## The Clark checks (Clark 1999), reported
Three seeds a map, years 100–500 (1200–1600), historical events on:
- `b_P` is the response of a settlement's log grain price to the log regional price, in a regression with settlement fixed effects and log local yield.
- `b_Y` is the response to log local yield in the same regression, where local yield is the harvest per cultivated cell.
- R² is the share of local log-price variation explained by the regional price alone. The regional price is the population-weighted mean grain price that year.

| Run | `b_P` (0.8–1.2; Clark 0.997) | `b_Y` (−0.15–0; Clark −0.025) | R² (≥ 0.60; Clark 0.86) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| map 1, seeds 1–3 | 0.984 / 0.985 / 0.991 | −0.007 / +0.002 / −0.006 | 0.98 / 0.98 / 0.98 |
| map 2, seeds 1–3 | 0.990 / 0.984 / 1.151 | −0.009 / −0.000 / +0.025 | 0.96 / 0.98 / 0.66 |
| map 3, seeds 1–3 | 0.985 / 0.987 / 0.986 | −0.002 / +0.006 / +0.010 | 0.98 / 0.98 / 0.98 |

- **`b_P` and R².** Both are inside the ranges in every run. Local prices follow the regional price almost one for one, as Clark found.
- **`b_Y`.** It is within ±0.03 of zero. It is slightly positive, outside the strict 0 bound, in 5 of the 9 runs. The model has no local weather: a place's yield per cell moves only when it changes which fields it works, or in the regional famine, which the regional price absorbs. So the yield effect is not identified, and the small positive values are noise around zero. Gating `b_Y` at S2 will need local yield shocks, or a range that admits zero noise.
- **Why R² is so high.** In the model, the common swings (the famine, the plague, growth) are the only large movements in grain prices.

## Price gaps against transport, reported
On the grain arcs carrying at least one unit (about 25,000 a year at 1310, 1316 and 1500), the median price gap is **0.20–0.28 of the transport price**. In Samuelson's exact equilibrium the gap equals transport on every route used. With the entropy term, and the W premium on big markets (decision 4), grain also moves on smaller gaps. As β/τ grows the gap tends to transport.

## Convergence
- **Unmatched demand,** as a share of demand at the buyers some supplier reaches, medians over the run: grain 0.3–0.5%, salt 0.3–4% (one run 28%), iron 1–4%, stone 0–3%, imports 0%.
- **The year after the famine.** Grain converges poorly that year (residual 0.3–0.5), because the prices warm-start from the famine level. Demand that no supplier reaches is reported, not counted.

## Tools
- `hinterlands prices --params <file with market_shadow true> [--from Y] [--arcs y,y]` dumps prices, yields, solver residuals and grain arcs.
- `tools/market/clark.py` computes the checks above.
- `tools/market/price_map.py` draws the maps.
