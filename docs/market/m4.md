# v7 M4: the hunger response and the famine shape (D101)

## Built
- **`hunger_tolerance`.** A place whose food ratio is between it and 1 stops growing but sheds nobody. Below it, D34 sheds a third of the gap below the tolerance each year.
- **The famine follows the record:** yields −40%, −60% and −10% in 1315, 1316 and 1317 (`famine_loss`, `famine_loss_2`, `famine_loss_3`). Checked on map 1 seed 1: the harvest is 0.61, 0.39 and 0.91 of 1314's.

## Findings
1. **The tolerance moves the dip, but only to the edge of the target at the bottom of its range.** At 0.80 the dip is 0.16–0.17, and 0.85 gives 0.18–0.19. Shedding is the only path by which people are lost in the famine: negative pull draws no migrants, and the unfed are not removed.
2. **The weather cost hardly moves:** population at year 200 is 0.84–0.87 of the same seed without weather, against M3's 0.83–0.86 (20 seeds). The rest of the cost is not shedding. Bad years stop growth, good years are capped at `food_cap`, and growth is lost in both directions (Jensen's inequality on the growth curve).
3. **Spacing falls.** Places that once shed in bad years now hold on. More mid-sized places survive, and they sit closer together: spacing on map 1 passes in 14/20 seeds, against 19/20 at M3.
4. **Roads on map 3** pass in 13/20 against 15/20. This check has sat at 15–16/20 since M2, so it may be noise near the threshold.

## Second build: temporary and chronic hunger (D102)
- **Built.** Growth and chronic shedding (a third of `1 − average` a year) use the mean food ratio over the last `hunger_years` (5) years. Acute shedding uses the year's gap below `hunger_tolerance`. The larger of the two applies.
- **Results, 5 seeds a map:**

| Run | Famine dip (0.10–0.16) | Recovered | Pop. 200 / no weather (≥ 0.85) | Spacing | Roads | Failing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D102, tolerance 0.80 | 0.24 / 0.24 / 0.24 | 19 years | 0.80 / 0.82 / 0.83 | 5 / 5 / 4 of 5 | 4 / 5 / 4 of 5 | none |
| D102, tolerance 0.85 | 0.25 / 0.25 / 0.25 | 20 years | 0.80 / 0.82 / 0.83 | 5 / 5 / 4 of 5 | 2 / 5 / 5 of 5 | roads, map 1 |
| Experiment: an acute year enters the average at the tolerance | 0.16 / 0.15 / 0.17 | 14 years | 0.80 / 0.82 / 0.83 | 5 / 3 / 5 of 5 | 3 / 5 / 5 of 5 | over 2,000, top-10 vs off and roads on map 1; spacing on map 2 |

- **Findings:**
  1. **Spacing and roads pass under D102.** Chronic shedding again removes places that stay short.
  2. **The famine dip gets worse, 0.22 → 0.24.**
     - After 1315–17 the five-year average stays below 1 for five years: (0.6 + 0.4 + 0.9 + 1 + 1) / 5 ≈ 0.78. So chronic shedding goes on after the famine, on top of the acute losses.
     - The past ratios were measured against the population before it shed, so the average counts the same shortfall twice.
  3. **Entering an acute year at the tolerance** removes the double count and brings the dip to 0.15–0.17. But spacing fails again on map 2: famine years no longer count against marginal places.
  4. **The weather cost does not move in any run** (0.80–0.83). Averaging smooths the growth term. But bad years now stay in the average for five years, as chronic shedding, so the loss is paid later rather than avoided.
- **Kept on main:** D102 as decided (tolerance 0.80). The experiment is not committed. The golden is not moved.
