# v7 M0: marketplaces diagnostic (report)

This is a diagnostic with no feedback. `hinterlands marketplaces --map-seed M --seed S --at 300,450` runs the defaults (v6 with W2) with the inert shadow market on (D77). The golden is unchanged (`1faa5c46e3908124`). For each settlement it computes:
- **Turnover under seller routing.** Each place sells its rent (`market_share × harvest`) plus what its people do not eat (`(1 − market_share) × harvest − need`, if positive). This goes to markets by `W^α exp(−β c)`, its own market by `home_weight`, and is valued at the market's shadow grain price.
- **Income under marketplace accounting:**
  - a toll on turnover (2%, 5%, 10%);
  - rents received, the same rent routed by the same weights and valued at the payer's price;
  - fees (`handling_margin × through + transship_margin × transship`) at its own price;
  - goods sales.
- **Its food bill:** its people's need beyond what its own fields give after rent, at its own shadow price.
- **The break-even toll:** (bill − rents − fees − goods) / turnover, zero when the rest already covers the bill.

Analysis: `tools/market/m0.py`. 3 seeds a map, years 300 and 450.

## Turnover against today's `market_in`
- **Spearman 0.955–0.982 in all 18 run-years. Pre-registered ≥ 0.8: MET.**
- **Rank-size slope** (log value against log rank, places with value ≥ 1): turnover −1.84 to −2.08, `market_in` −1.83 to −2.08. They match within 0.03 in every run.
- **Top-10 overlap:** 10/10 in 15 of 18, 9/10 in 2, and 8/10 in 1.

Seller routing concentrates exactly as `market_in` does. That is expected, since it uses the same weights. The point is that turnover is a quantity a market can be paid on.

**Most towns over 2,000 break even at a toll of 10% or less (91–93%), rents included. Pre-registered: MET.**

**What the numbers say:**
- **Rents carry the towns.** In towns over 2,000 the means are: turnover 4,414, rents 4,058, fees 356, goods 51, food bill 3,712. Turnover is mostly the rent grain itself, because villages cultivate for their need plus rent with little true surplus. So rents alone cover most towns' food, and a toll is a small extra.
- **The towns that fail at 10%** (12 of 146 town-years) have bills just above their rents and little trade passing through. They need tolls of 11–24%, and one needs 52% (map 2 seed 1, a town of 2,408 with 1,125 in rents and a bill of 1,755).
- **Ports and transshipment towns earn the largest fees** (up to 1,155 a year). They are among the few where fees matter.
- **The 24–27% of places over 500 that do not break even** at 10% are mostly villages of 500–2,000 whose people outgrow their fields. Under marketplaces they would buy from their own farmers' surplus or shed people.

## For M1
- **The design is viable on these numbers.** Rents give towns the income to buy their food, and turnover has the same rank structure as v5's inflow.
- **Two things for the note:**
  1. Since turnover ≈ rent, W growing on turnover is almost W growing on rents. The feedback that concentrates towns stays that of the rent routing, as now.
  2. A toll of 2–5% would cover most of the shortfall cases, so tolls need not be large.
