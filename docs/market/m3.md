# v7 M3: stores at markets (work in progress)

Built as briefed (D94), behind `market_stores`, which is off while M3 is iterated. The golden is unchanged (`835e8695b67538f7`).
- **Each market with W ≥ `market_min` holds a store.**
  - It buys when its price is below its ten-year average less the holding margin h (0.15 before year 250, 0.10 after).
  - It sells when the price is above the average, in proportion to the gap (`store_gain`), at most `store_cap` × its turnover, within what it holds or has room and budget for.
  - The store's trade enters the market's supply and demand inside the price solve. Takings are next year's income, and `store_loss` applies.
- **Price gaps on market pairs** are now recorded (`State::market_pairs`).
- **New reports in `validate`:** detrended price autocorrelation, store carry-over after cheap years, and pair gap over transport.

## Findings (5 seeds a map; full numbers in the session log)
1. **As briefed, stores barely act.** Famine dip 0.18–0.19 (0.18–0.20 without stores); autocorrelation 0.24–0.28.
   - **Cause:** grain prices rise steadily as the region fills (map 1 seed 1: 0.35 at year 200, 0.8 at 214, 1.3 at 400). The ten-year average lags that trend, so the price stays above it and the stores sell out before 1315. They hold 33–41% of a year's need around year 200, and none by 210.
2. **A sell band** (sell above the average plus 1–2 holding margins) does not help: the trend crosses it. **A shorter average** (3–5 years) is worse: the stores rarely see a "cheap" price to buy at.
3. **Selling into a spike** (`store_spike`: sell only when the price is also above last year's by the holding margin) keeps the stores through the trend. Stores hold 0.26–0.33 of a year's need before 1315 and are drawn to 0.05 by 1317.
   - But the famine dip is unchanged at 0.17–0.20, and autocorrelation is 0.14–0.24.
4. **Why the stores do not reach the famine: villages have no money.**
   - A market store sells only into its market, to the market people and the ports.
   - The famine starves villagers, whose own harvest falls 40%. A village has no budget to buy with, because its farmers' sales revenue is not kept and nothing carries value between years.
   - Relief is unpaid but draws only on surplus and village stores.
   - Letting relief draw on market stores too (`relief_market_stores`, experiment) does not help: in 1315–17 the market towns are short themselves, so their stores are not offered. Village deficit met in the famine is 0–3%.
5. **Pair price gaps** are about 2.1–2.4 × transport. Small market-to-market flows carry the entropy margin `ln(x)/θ` above transport.

## Design question for the designer
For stores to carry grain to where the famine bites, the people short of grain must be able to buy it. Options:
- **A. Village purses.** Farmers keep their sales revenue as a store of value, a money stock in grain units carried across years with a loss rate. They spend it on grain in a dearth and on goods otherwise. This is the first stock of value in the model.
- **B. Market stores sell to deficit villages on credit,** repaid from later harvests: a debt stock.
- **C. Market stores open to relief at a price paid from the market's own budget** (charity by the town's lords and church): no new stock.

`market_stores` stays off until this is decided.
