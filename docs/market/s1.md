## What the prices show (year 450, 1550; 3 seeds a map; buyers that some supplier reaches)
| Good | Towns (median delivered price) | Sites (supply prices) | Unmet demand a year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Salt | 0.59–0.85 | 0.56–0.84 | 530–970 |
| **Iron** | **22–80** | **21–86** | **1,900–2,240** |
| Stone | 0.71–1.02 | 0.22–1.00 | 40–340 |
| Imports | 0.98–0.99 | 1 (the ports) | 350–640 |

- **Iron is very scarce at the v2 demand.** About 0.02 per person, some 2,300 a year, against mine output of about 150, which the coppice rule (D51) limits to the regrowth of the holder's woods. At an elasticity of 0.5 the price rises 22–80 times before demand meets supply, and most iron demand still goes unmet.
- **The iron rent makes mines large.** The holder receives the supply price on every unit. **Specialists at year 500 have a median of 1,483 / 1,649 / 2,799 people (maximum 7,920),** against v5's 64 / 43 / 105 (maximum 219).
- **What it does to map 2.** That map has the most mines, and their growth crowds its market spacing below the 75% line. It stays at 19/20 on "largest not a specialist".
- **Salt and imports** are close to their old value. Stone is near 1 where it is supplied.
- **An edge case.** One run (map 3, seed 2) had no working stone sites at that year, and its buyers' stone price fell to the 0.001 floor. The price is undefined there and should be reported as unreached, not as a price.

## Price gaps against transport (goods arcs carrying at least 0.1, year 450, seed 1)
The median gap between delivered and supply price, as a share of the transport price, is **0.91 / 1.06 / 0.85**: close to Samuelson's equality on the routes used. The spread is wide (p10 −3, p90 +3), as the entropy term allows.

## Options (the designer's to choose)
1. **Re-scale iron demand to coppiced supply.** `demand_iron` was set in v2 before the coppice rule halved iron output. Lowering it brings the iron price, and the mines' rent, back to the same scale as the other goods.
2. **Raise iron's elasticity.** Above 0.5, demand gives way sooner, so prices stay lower when supply is short.
3. **Cap a supplier's price, or its rent.** For example, at a few times the old value, with the rest of the iron demand left unmet as in D41. This is a new rule.
4. **Re-tune the founding values with goods on,** as was done in D66, keeping the demand.
