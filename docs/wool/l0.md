# L0: wool and land use, the diagnostic (D105)

A probe with no feedback: `hinterlands wool` (CLI only; the core and the golden are unchanged), aggregated by `tools/wool/l0.py`. It covers 3 seeds on each generated map plus 3 on Kent (map-bridges), at years 200, 240 (1340), 300 (1400) and 400 (1500), with three trial wool prices.

## Findings
1. **A gives conversion after the plague, as the proposal intends:**
   - pasture cells go from 36 in 1340 to 67 in 1400 (Kent 28 → 75), and back to 17 by 1500 (Kent 11) as people return;
   - wool coin goes from 0.8× grain coin to 1.7× at £5.
2. **But A as specified puts pasture inward and on good land:**
   - A takes free cells nearest first, so its pasture sits near the settlements (share 0.25 in the nearest quintile in 1300, 0.02 in the farthest) and on the free cells of highest yield;
   - that would fail L1's gate that pasture lies outward and on poor land;
   - **a fix within A:** take free cells in order of lowest arable rent (poorest and farthest first), not nearest first. The labour limit is unchanged.
3. **B has von Thünen's shape but would starve the towns:**
   - pasture is outward (0.17 → 0.65 by cost quintile) and on poor land (yield < 0.3: 0.99; ≥ 0.7: 0.00);
   - but at £4–5 a sack 36–58% of claimed land converts, losing 19–41% of the harvest, which is 45–103% of the grain the markets receive;
   - B needs `convert_rate` and hysteresis at the least, and a check on town supply. The owner's condition for B ("L0 shows that the towns keep their grain") is **not met** at historical prices.
4. **Wool as the main export:**
   - under A at £4–5, wool's coin is 0.63–0.81× grain's before 1348, so 39–45% of coin entering (Kent 35–52%), against the proposal's "above half";
   - after 1348 it is 1.3–1.7× (57–62%);
   - at Clark's £9–10 it is above half throughout.
5. **Relative prices fell against wool after the plague, not for it.**
   - Clark's series: wool per lb against wheat per bushel was about 0.92 in the 1300s and 0.67–0.68 from the 1340s to the 1440s.
   - The post-plague turn to grass therefore came from labour and population, not from relative prices. A carries that; B would need falling grain prices or rising labour costs.
6. **`rho_wool` should be about 50, not 5.** With 5, carriage to the port costs pasture 1–3% of its value; with 50, almost nothing. Wool's handling income on the routes depends on it.
