# L2 (the murrain) and L4 (the view) (D112)

## L2: the murrain
- **Built:** in the Historical calendar, wool output from pasture is ×0.7 in 1315–17 and ×0.85 in 1318–22 (`murrain`, `murrain_loss` 0.3, `murrain_late_loss` 0.15).
- **The figures are approximate,** after Campbell 2000 and Slavin.
  - No clean English figure for flock losses in 1315–22 was found.
  - Slavin (2020, *Economic History Review*, from 7,871 manorial accounts) puts the wool crisis down mainly to recurrent sheep scab, which depressed flocks and wool output until the early 1330s ([Stirling repository](https://www.storre.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/30952/1/ehr.12969.pdf)).
  - The 1319–22 murrain was chiefly a cattle plague.
  - So the record may favour a longer, milder wool depression than the one built.

## L4: the view (new file `layers/wool.rs`, with small changes to the relief, countryside, hover and chronicle code)
- **Pasture and sheep come from the model's grazed land** (`Snapshot.pasture`). A snapshot from before L1, which carries none, falls back to the old rule (open land by the fields).
- **Wool to the ports** is a new route colour, fleece cream: the "Wool to the ports" layer, drawing the 40 largest flows.
- **The chronicle has two new kinds of line:**
  - "1420. The fields of Ashby are put down to grass." This is logged when a place puts at least 3 old fields (cleared cells) to grass in a year, at most once per 50 years a place. Map 1 seed 1 has 12 such lines over 500 years.
  - "1315. Murrain among the sheep of X." This is logged at the place with the most pasture.
